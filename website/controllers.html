Racing wheels, flight sticks and RC radios

Plug your wheel, stick or radio into the computer you play from and open MoonlightWeb in the browser: the game on your gaming PC sees it, force feedback included.

Two ways to reach the game

As an Xbox controller

The browser reads the device and the game receives an Xbox 360 controller: two sticks, two triggers and the buttons. EdgeTX and OpenTX radios and the Logitech G923 are recognized on plug-in; any other device is set up once with the wizard in Settings → Controllers.

As itself

From Chrome or Edge on a computer, switch on “As itself” in Settings → Controllers. MoonlightWeb’s own host recreates the real device on the gaming PC, under its own name: every axis and button, the G923’s clutch, all eight channels of a TX12, and force feedback for wheels.

Devices

  • RC radios — Radiomaster TX12, TX16S, Zorro, Boxer, Pocket, MT12, Jumper, BetaFPV, and FrSky or Flysky radios under EdgeTX or OpenTX, in USB Joystick mode. Tested in Liftoff with a TX12.
  • Logitech wheels — the G923 for Xbox and PC, with force feedback, tested in Assetto Corsa Competizione; the G920 too. The G29 and the G923 for PlayStation are recreated, without force feedback yet.
  • Direct-drive wheels that speak USB PID (Moza, Simucube, Fanatec…) — supported, not tried on one yet.
  • Flight sticks, HOTAS and pedals — set up once with the wizard, or sent as themselves.

On the gaming PC

  • Windows (x64) — the installer sets up the MoonlightWeb Virtual HID driver; Secure Boot stays on. A game left running keeps its wheel and force feedback between two streams for 10 minutes.
  • Linux — nothing more to install with MoonlightWeb’s package.
  • macOS — no controller reaches a Mac host: macOS lets no app create a virtual gamepad.
Sending a device as itself needs MoonlightWeb’s own host; a Sunshine, Apollo or Wolf host receives an Xbox controller.

Logitech, Radiomaster, Moza, Simucube, Fanatec, EdgeTX and the games named here belong to their owners.
